# Visual inspiration

These images and animations shaped how GeoHand *looks and moves*. None of them is included in the tool, and none is a source for any fact. The cited science references are in `src/content/references.js` and `docs/sources.md`.

Creators are given where the image itself names them. Rows marked "to confirm" need a link from whoever supplied the image.

| # | What it shows | Creator / source | Informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cone-shaped cut-away with thickness labels (lithosphere 80 km, mantle 2,800 km, outer core 2,300 km, inner core 1,200 km) | to confirm | a clean cut-away sector with each layer labelled by thickness |
| 2 | "Planet Earth" pizza cartoon (crust and "core" only) | @FOXADHD (signed on the image) | tone only. The structure is a joke, not geology, and is not used. |
| 3 | Meridional cross-section, 65 Ma: mantle density anomaly (δρ %) with 440, 660 and 1,000 km depth rings | to confirm | colouring the cross-section by a measured property, with a scale bar and depth rings |
| 4 | Subduction animation: an ocean plate pushed under a continent, with arrows and captions | to confirm | motion with direction arrows and captions in sequence. Note: whole oceanic lithosphere subducts, not only the crust. |
| 5 | "Earth's Internal Structure": mechanical versus compositional layers, a "How do we know?" box, and a table of composition, state and share of radius and volume | Chris Rowan, 2016 (named on the image) | the layer card (state, composition, evidence) and the mechanical-versus-compositional idea |
| 6 | P-wave velocities: rays from an earthquake to seismograph stations ("ray-path timing is simplified") | to confirm | the planned earthquake view: P and S paths, stations and shadow zones, with an honest simplification note |
